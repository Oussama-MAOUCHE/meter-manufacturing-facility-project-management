# Representative Issue — Design Interface, Delay and Change Follow-up

This example shows how the project-management work combined technical understanding, schedule awareness, stakeholder coordination and contractual follow-up.

## Situation

During execution, the project faced two related pressures:

- delayed procurement / availability of the steel structure;
- a design-interface problem involving the freight-lift area and the foundations.

The design issue could not be treated as an isolated drawing correction because it affected execution, technical-control review, complementary works and the schedule.

## Follow-up

My documented involvement included:

- tracking the issue and its effect on progress;
- coordinating with the contractor and engineering parties;
- following technical-control feedback;
- documenting the problem and related delay context;
- following the study correction and modified-plan path;
- tracking complementary work and schedule implications;
- following contractor commitments, formal notices and agreed actions.

## Change and schedule implications

The issue fed into a wider change-control context involving additional/complementary works and time impacts.

The project records also document work-order / stop-resume controls in the later delivery scope, illustrating how technical changes and execution readiness were connected to formal project controls.

## Lesson carried into the later scope

The experience from this phase influenced how the later scope was handled: the engineering-consultant responsibilities were broadened around study adaptation, and earlier identification of plan gaps was requested to reduce the chance of discovering similar conflicts only after execution had advanced.

This is one example of how technical coordination, schedule impact and contract/change controls interacted during delivery.
